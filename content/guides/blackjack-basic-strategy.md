Basic strategy is the one piece of blackjack knowledge that pays for itself. It is a short table of decisions – hit, stand, double or split – for every combination of your hand and the dealer's up-card, derived from the exact probabilities of the game. Play it perfectly on a six-deck table where the dealer stands on soft 17 and blackjack pays 3 to 2, and the house edge falls to about 0.41%. Play by feel and you are usually giving away 2% or more on every bet. You can try the whole chart with hints on our [free blackjack game](/table-games/blackjack), which uses exactly these rules.

## The rules this chart assumes

Charts differ slightly between rule sets, so it matters which one you learn. This one is for:

- six decks, dealer **stands on soft 17**;
- blackjack pays 3 to 2;
- double on any two cards, **including after a split**;
- split up to three hands, split aces receive one card;
- no surrender, no insurance.

If your table lets the dealer hit soft 17, three cells change: double 11 against an ace, double soft 18 against a 2, and double soft 19 against a 6. Everything else stays the same.

## Hard totals

A hard total is any hand without an ace counted as 11. These rows do most of the work.

| Your total | Dealer 2–3 | Dealer 4–6 | Dealer 7–A |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 or less | Hit | Hit | Hit |
| 9 | Hit vs 2, double vs 3 | Double | Hit |
| 10 | Double | Double | Double vs 7–9, hit vs 10/A |
| 11 | Double | Double | Double vs 7–10, hit vs A |
| 12 | Hit | Stand | Hit |
| 13–16 | Stand | Stand | Hit |
| 17+ | Stand | Stand | Stand |

The logic rests on one fact about the dealer: showing a 2 through 6, the dealer busts between 35% and 42% of the time; showing a 7 or higher, far less often. So with a stiff hand of 12 to 16 you stand against a weak up-card and let the dealer bust, but you hit against a strong one because standing on 15 against a 10 wins only about 23% of the time, while hitting wins a little more often. The exception is 12 against a 2 or 3, where the dealer busts slightly less often and your chance of catching a small card makes hitting better.

Doubling 10 and 11 is where the money is made. You are a strong favourite to finish with 20 or 21, so you want twice the stake out there. Doubling 9 against a 3 to 6 and declining to double 11 against an ace (the dealer stands on soft 17, which makes the ace up-card slightly stronger) are the two cells people most often get wrong.

## Soft totals

A soft hand contains an ace counted as 11, so you cannot bust with one more card. That freedom turns many of these hands into doubles against a weak dealer.

| Your hand | Action |
|---|---|
| A-2, A-3 | Double vs 5–6, otherwise hit |
| A-4, A-5 | Double vs 4–6, otherwise hit |
| A-6 | Double vs 3–6, otherwise hit |
| A-7 | Double vs 3–6, stand vs 2, 7, 8, hit vs 9, 10, A |
| A-8, A-9 | Stand |

Soft 18 is the famous trap. It looks like a good hand, but against a 9, 10 or ace the dealer finishes with 19 or better too often, and hitting – which can only improve you or leave you with a hard total you would play normally – is worth more than standing. Against a 3 to 6 you double it, because you are likely to win anyway and the extra card can only help or leave you with a decent hand.

## Pairs

| Pair | Action |
|---|---|
| A-A, 8-8 | Always split |
| 2-2, 3-3, 7-7 | Split vs 2–7, otherwise hit |
| 6-6 | Split vs 2–6, otherwise hit |
| 4-4 | Split vs 5–6, otherwise hit |
| 9-9 | Split vs 2–6 and 8–9, stand vs 7, 10, A |
| 5-5 | Never split – play as 10 (double vs 2–9) |
| 10-10 | Never split – stand on 20 |

Splitting aces turns one awkward 12 into two hands that each start with the best card in the deck. Splitting 8s turns the worst total in the game, 16, into two playable hands; it still loses money against a 10 or ace, just less than standing or hitting on 16 would. Splitting 4s is only right because double after split is allowed: 4-4 against a 5 or 6 becomes two chances to catch a 5, 6 or 7 and double.

## What mistakes cost

Not every error is equal. Standing on 16 against a 10 instead of hitting costs about 1% of the bet – small. Failing to double 11 against a 6 costs over 20% of the bet. Splitting 10s against a 6 costs around 15%. So if you only learn a few rows, learn the doubles and the two "always split" pairs; those are the decisions with the largest swings.
