In a slot with **tumbling reels**, a winning spin is not the end of the round. The winning symbols are removed, the remaining symbols fall down to fill the gaps, and new symbols drop in from above. If that creates another win, it happens again – all from the single spin you paid for.

The mechanic goes by many names: *tumble* at Pragmatic Play, *avalanche* at Thunderkick, *cascades*, *drops*, *collapsing reels* or *super cascades* elsewhere.

## Why tumbles matter

Tumbles turn one spin into a **chain**. That chain is where most cascading slots hide their value, usually through something that grows with each step:

- **Progressive multipliers.** In [Esqueleto Explosivo](/slots/thunderkick/esqueleto-explosivo), the Mucho Multiplier climbs with every consecutive drop. The [Crystal Quest](/slots/thunderkick/crystal-quest-frostlands) games use uncapped multipliers that keep rising through the chain.
- **Multiplier symbols collected at the end.** In [Gates of Olympus](/slots/pragmatic-play/gates-of-olympus) and [Sweet Bonanza](/slots/pragmatic-play/sweet-bonanza), multiplier orbs or bombs land during tumbles and are added up once the chain ends.
- **Board-clearing tricks.** Hacksaw's *Super Cascades* in [Ze Zeus](/slots/hacksaw-gaming/ze-zeus) remove every symbol of a winning type, not just the winning group, which clears space much faster.
- **Growing grids.** In [Fire in the Hole xBomb](/slots/nolimit-city/fire-in-the-hole-xbomb), every collapse opens another row of the mine.

## Tumbles and pay systems

Cascades work with any pay system, but they shine with **scatter pays** (8+ matching symbols anywhere) and **cluster pays** (groups of touching symbols), because removing a group naturally creates space for the next. Read more in [paylines vs ways vs cluster pays](/guides/paylines-vs-ways-vs-cluster-pays).

## What tumbles do to volatility

A long tumble chain with a rising multiplier is how many cascading slots reach their max win, but such chains are rare. Most spins tumble once or not at all. Games that rely on multipliers *after* the chain – like Gates of Olympus – tend to be very high volatility. Games with small, steady drop multipliers – like the original Esqueleto Explosivo, rated low volatility by Thunderkick – feel much calmer.
