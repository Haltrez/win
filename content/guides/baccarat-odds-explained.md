Baccarat has no decisions after the bet, so its entire strategy is knowing which bet to make. The numbers below are for standard Punto Banco with eight decks, the version you'll find in almost every casino and on our [free baccarat game](/table-games/baccarat). They settle the question quickly: Banker is the best bet, Player is close behind, and Tie is the one to leave alone.

## How often each side wins

Because the third-card tableau is fixed, the probabilities can be calculated exactly. Over a full eight-deck shoe:

| Outcome | Probability |
|---|---|
| Banker wins | 45.86% |
| Player wins | 44.62% |
| Tie | 9.52% |

The Banker hand wins more often because it acts last. Whether the Banker draws a third card depends on the Player's third card, which gives the Banker side a built-in information edge – the same reason the dealer has an edge in blackjack by acting after the player.

## Why Banker pays 0.95 to 1

If Banker paid even money, a Banker bet would have a *player* advantage of about 1.2%. No casino offers that, so a 5% commission is deducted from Banker wins. Here is the house edge of each bet with the standard payouts:

| Bet | Pays | House edge |
|---|---|---|
| Banker | 0.95 to 1 | 1.06% |
| Player | 1 to 1 | 1.24% |
| Tie | 8 to 1 | 14.36% |
| Tie | 9 to 1 | 4.84% |

Work through the Banker figure yourself: you win 0.95 units 45.86% of the time, lose 1 unit 44.62% of the time, and push on the 9.52% of ties. That is 0.4357 − 0.4462 = −0.0106 units per coup, or −1.06%. Measured only over hands that are decided (ties excluded), the edges are 1.17% on Banker and 1.36% on Player, which is how some books quote them.

The Tie bet is the outlier. A 9.52% chance paid at 8 to 1 returns 9 × 0.0952 = 0.857 units per unit bet – a 14.36% edge, in the same league as the worst slot machines. Even at 9 to 1 it is four times worse than Banker.

## No-commission and side bets

"No-commission" baccarat sounds like a gift: Banker pays 1 to 1. The catch is usually that a Banker win with a three-card total of 6 pays only 1 to 2. That one exception happens often enough to lift the Banker edge to 1.46%, worse than paying the commission. Other variants, such as paying a Banker 7 win at 1 to 2 or 1 to 4, carry edges between 1% and 2.5%, so read the table card before assuming "no commission" means a better deal.

Side bets – Player Pair, Banker Pair, Perfect Pair, Big and Small, Dragon Bonus and the rest – range from about 2.6% to over 17% house edge. They add action and nothing else.

## The shoe has no memory

Baccarat tables are famous for their roadmaps: the bead plate, big road, big eye boy and so on, all tracking streaks and patterns. They are a pleasant ritual, and we show a simple bead plate on our free table because it is part of the game's atmosphere. But a shoe does not remember what it dealt. The chance that Banker wins the next coup is 45.86% whether Banker has won once or eight times in a row. Betting "with the shoe" or against a run has exactly the same expectation as betting at random, minus the time it takes.

Card counting in baccarat is technically possible but worthless: the best published systems produce a tiny edge on a handful of coups per shoe, far below what any practical bet could exploit.

## What this means for your money

- Bet Banker. The 5% commission is already priced in, and it is still the lowest edge on the table.
- Avoid Tie and the side bets.
- Flat-bet. Since every coup is independent, no progression changes the long-run result; it only changes how large your swings are.
- Treat a 1.06% edge as the cost of the entertainment: around one unit lost for every 95 units wagered, on average, over the long run.
