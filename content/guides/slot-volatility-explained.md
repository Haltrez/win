**Volatility** (also called variance) describes *how* a slot pays out its RTP. Two slots can both have a 96% RTP and feel completely different:

- A **low volatility** slot pays small wins often. Your balance drifts down slowly with frequent bumps.
- A **high volatility** slot pays rarely, but some wins are very large. Your balance can fall steadily for hundreds of spins, then jump.

## Same RTP, different experience

Imagine two games, each returning 96%. Game A pays 1.5x your stake roughly every third spin. Game B pays almost nothing for long stretches but has a bonus that occasionally returns 500x. Both average out to 96% – over millions of spins. Over your 300-spin session, Game B is far more likely to leave you with nothing, and also the only one that could produce a life-changing hit.

## How to recognise volatility

- **Max win:** caps of 10,000x+ almost always mean high volatility. See [how max win works](/guides/how-max-win-works).
- **Hit frequency:** if published, a lower percentage usually means higher volatility.
- **Feature dependence:** if almost all the game's value comes from free spins, it's volatile.
- **Provider rating:** most studios publish a volatility rating (often 1–5 or low/medium/high).

## Choosing the right level

| Your situation | Sensible volatility |
|---|---|
| Small budget, want longer sessions | Low to medium |
| Want to understand features before playing | Any – try it in demo first |
| Comfortable losing the full budget for a shot at big wins | High |

Whatever you choose, set a budget first. High volatility does not mean "due to pay" – every spin is independent.

The best way to feel volatility is to play 200–300 demo spins of a [low volatility slot](/best/low-volatility-slots) and then a [high volatility slot](/best/high-volatility-slots). The difference is obvious within minutes, and it costs nothing.
