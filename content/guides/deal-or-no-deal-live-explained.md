**Deal or No Deal Live** is Evolution's live version of the TV show, launched in 2019 as the world's first 24/7 game show. Each game has three parts: a qualifying round, an optional top-up round, and the main game with 16 briefcases and the banker's offers.

It's live and real money only, with **no demo**. For free play, see our [slot demos](/slots).

## 1. Qualifying round

You bet to spin a three-reel **bank vault wheel**. The aim is to line up **three gold segments**. You choose how much of the vault is gold (more gold = easier to qualify, but a higher bet), and you can spin until the round closes. Qualify and you're in the main game; miss and your bet is lost.

## 2. Top-up round

Before the main game, a **slots-style top-up** lets you increase the values in the briefcases: a reel with random multipliers is spun, and the multiplier boosts the cases. Optional, but it raises the prizes you're playing for.

## 3. The main game

16 briefcases are opened in rounds, each holding a cash value based on your qualifying bet. After each round, the **banker** makes an offer to buy your case. You have three offers in total, and the live host asks the question: **Deal or No Deal?**

- **Deal** – you take the banker's offer and the game ends.
- **No Deal** – play on; your prize is whatever's in the last briefcase.

While the game runs you can also bet on side bets, such as whether the final case will be higher than the banker's offer.

## RTP

Independent sources report **about 95.42%** for Deal or No Deal Live. The qualifying round carries most of the house edge; once you're in the main game, taking or refusing the banker's offer is a judgement call, not a way to change the long-run return.

## Who it suits

Slower and more tense than the wheel games – one game can take several minutes. If you like the TV show, it's faithful. If you want quick spins, [Dream Catcher](/guides/dream-catcher-explained) or [Crazy Time](/guides/crazy-time-explained) move faster.
