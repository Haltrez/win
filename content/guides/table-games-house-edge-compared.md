Every casino game is built so that the casino keeps a slice of the money bet over time. That slice is the **house edge**, and knowing it for each game is the most useful fact a player can carry into a casino. Below are the figures for the five table games you can play free on this site, the rules behind them, and what they mean in money terms.

## The numbers

House edge is quoted per unit bet with the best bet or optimal play; RTP (return to player) is simply 100% minus the edge.

| Game | Rules / bet | House edge | RTP | Skill? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Blackjack](/table-games/blackjack) | 6 decks, dealer stands on soft 17, 3:2, double after split, basic strategy | 0.41% | 99.59% | Yes |
| [Jacks or Better 9/6](/table-games/video-poker) | Optimal holds, five coins | 0.46% | 99.54% | Yes |
| [Baccarat](/table-games/baccarat) | Banker bet, 8 decks | 1.06% | 98.94% | Bet choice |
| [Casino Hold'em](/table-games/casino-holdem) | Standard paytable, optimal call/fold | 2.16% of ante | 97.84% | Yes |
| [European roulette](/table-games/roulette) | Any bet, single zero | 2.70% | 97.30% | No |

For context, the same games have expensive cousins: American double-zero roulette has a 5.26% edge, the baccarat Tie bet 14.36%, a 6/5 blackjack table (blackjack pays 6 to 5) about 1.8% even with perfect play, and an 8/5 Jacks or Better machine 2.70%. Reading the table card before sitting down is worth more than any system.

## What the edge means in money

The house edge is a rate, so it multiplies by how much you wager, not how much you bring. Play roulette at 10 a spin for 100 spins and you have wagered 1,000; the expected loss is 2.7% of that, 27. Play blackjack at the same stake for 100 hands with basic strategy and the expected loss is about 4. Over a weekend that difference compounds.

Two things complicate the comparison:

- **Speed.** A busy roulette table deals perhaps 40 spins an hour; video poker can run 600 hands an hour. A 0.46% edge at 600 hands an hour costs about as much per hour as a 2.7% edge at 100 spins. Hands per hour matter as much as the edge.
- **Variance.** A low edge does not mean small swings. Video poker's return leans on a royal flush that arrives once in 40,000 hands; most sessions lose. Even-money roulette bets, with a worse edge, produce much gentler results over an evening.

## Casino Hold'em's two numbers

Casino Hold'em is quoted two ways. The 2.16% edge is measured against the ante alone. But optimal play means calling, and betting two more units, on about 82% of hands, so the average amount at risk per hand is roughly 2.6 units. Measured against all money wagered the edge – called the **element of risk** – is 0.82%. That makes the game cheaper per unit bet than its headline figure suggests, and more expensive per hand than blackjack because the average bet is larger.

## Where skill moves the number

The table above assumes optimal play, and three of the five games punish anything less:

- **Blackjack.** Basic strategy versus typical recreational play is worth about 2%. The expensive mistakes are not doubling 10 and 11, not splitting aces and 8s, and standing on soft 18 against a 9, 10 or ace. Our [blackjack basic strategy guide](/guides/blackjack-basic-strategy) covers the full chart.
- **Video poker.** A player who keeps kickers and chases straights over low pairs can turn a 99.54% machine into a 97% one. The ranked hold list in our [Jacks or Better strategy guide](/guides/jacks-or-better-strategy) is the fix.
- **Casino Hold'em.** Folding too often is the common error. Fold only around 18% of hands – two low cards that missed the flop with no draw.

Baccarat and roulette involve no decisions after the bet, so there is nothing to get wrong except the choice of bet. In baccarat that choice (Banker, not Tie) is worth 13% of your stake; see [baccarat odds explained](/guides/baccarat-odds-explained). In roulette every bet on a single-zero wheel is identical at 2.70%; the only choice is between a double-zero and a single-zero table, or a table with la partage, which halves the edge on even-money bets to 1.35%.

## Table games versus slots

Most video slots return between 94% and 97%, a 3–6% house edge, and some land-based machines pay less. Table games played well cost a quarter to a tenth as much per unit wagered. What the slot edge buys is the chance of a very large win from a tiny stake – 5,000× or 10,000× maximum wins that no table game offers – plus bonus features and far more variety. If you are choosing purely on cost, table games win; if you are choosing on what a 0.20 stake can turn into, slots do.
