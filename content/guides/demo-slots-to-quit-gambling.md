**Short answer:** for some people, yes, as a temporary step and not as a cure. Some gambling addiction specialists suggest that people stuck in compulsive, high-stakes play first switch from real money to demo mode, then stop the demos too. The idea is to step down instead of stopping cold, with money taken out of the picture straight away. It works best as part of a plan agreed with a specialist, and it isn't right for everyone.

If you're reading this because gambling is costing you more than you can afford, you don't have to work it out alone. Free, confidential help is listed at the bottom of this page.

## Why a step-down instead of stopping cold?

For some people, stopping all at once is very hard to stick to. The routine of playing, the sounds and the anticipation of the bonus become part of daily life, and when all of that disappears overnight, the urge to go back can be strong.

Demo mode keeps the routine for a short while but takes away the part that does the damage: real money. Demo slots are the providers' own games played with a play-money balance. Nothing can be deposited, lost or withdrawn. The aim is to break the link between the game and your bank account first, then let go of the game itself.

## When this is not a good idea

Demo mode as a step-down isn't for everyone. Skip it, and talk to a specialist about other options, if:

- **you've already stopped gambling.** Don't start playing demos "just for fun". Many treatment programmes, including Gamblers Anonymous, recommend avoiding all gambling-style games, free ones included.
- **demo play makes you want to deposit.** If a big demo win or a near miss sends you looking for a casino, the step-down isn't working. Stop the demos too.
- **your specialist has recommended stopping completely.** Their plan comes first.
- **you're under 18.**

## A step-down plan, as an example

Every plan should be adjusted with a specialist. A typical shape looks like this.

**1. Block real-money gambling first.** Before you switch to demos, make sure you can't slip back with one click:

- sign up to a self-exclusion scheme (GAMSTOP in the UK, Spelpaus in Sweden, OASIS in Germany, or the self-exclusion tool at each casino you use);
- install blocking software such as Gamban or BetBlocker;
- turn on your bank's gambling block if it offers one;
- close your casino accounts.

**2. Switch to demo mode only, with limits.** Agree a fixed amount of time, for example a set session on set days, and stick to it. Use a timer. Don't use your old stake sizes, and don't chase bonus rounds.

**3. Cut it down week by week.** Shorter sessions, fewer days. Fill the freed-up time with something you already enjoy: exercise, friends, a game that isn't a gambling game.

**4. Stop the demos.** The step-down ends here. Delete apps and bookmarks, and keep the blocking tools on.

**5. Tell someone.** A partner, friend or support group makes it far easier to stay on track and to notice if things slip.

## Signs the step-down isn't working

- You're spending more time on demos than you planned, or hiding it.
- You're thinking about what a demo win "would have paid".
- You're looking up casino bonuses or trying to get round your blocks.
- You feel low, anxious or irritable when you aren't playing.

If any of these sound familiar, stop the demos and contact one of the services below.
