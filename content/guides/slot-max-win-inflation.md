A slot's **max win** is the largest amount it can pay on one round, written as a multiple of your stake. Ten years ago 5,000x was a big number. Today, many new releases open at 10,000x and some advertise 100,000x or more. We wanted to know how real that trend is, so we charted the official max win of every slot on Scatter Drop with a published cap and release date.

If you're new to the term, start with our guide to [how max win works](/guides/how-max-win-works).

## How we measured it

- **Official figures only.** Every max win comes from the provider's own game page or sheet. Slots without a published cap are left out: 761 of our 859 slots have one.
- **By release year.** We grouped slots by the year the provider released them. Years with fewer than five games are left out of the chart.
- **Our catalogue, not the whole market.** Our site covers 12 studios and leans towards popular and recent games, so treat this as a strong sample, not a census.

## What stands out

### The typical cap doubled, then settled

Up to 2019, the middle slot capped out at around 3,000x–5,000x. By 2022–2023 the median had doubled to 10,000x, and more than half of new releases advertised five figures. Since then the median has eased back to 6,000x–7,500x, but around half of new games still launch at 10,000x or more. In short, 10,000x went from a headline number to an ordinary one.

### The extreme end kept climbing

The biggest ceilings are a different story. Before 2019 none of our dated slots offered 50,000x. Since then they appear every year: [Dead or Alive 2](/slots/netent/dead-or-alive-2) at 111,111x, [San Quentin xWays](/slots/nolimit-city/san-quentin-xways) at 150,000x, [Tombstone RIP](/slots/nolimit-city/tombstone-rip) at 300,000x, and a 500,000x Tombstone edition in 2025. Pragmatic Play's Super Scatter editions brought 50,000x–100,000x caps to mainstream games like [Sweet Bonanza Super Scatter](/slots/pragmatic-play/sweet-bonanza-super-scatter). ## Why max wins went up

A slot's RTP is fixed: a 96% game pays back 96% of stakes over time, whatever its cap. Raising the max win doesn't add money; it moves money. More of the 96% is reserved for rare, enormous outcomes, so less is left for everyday wins. That is why bigger caps almost always come with [higher volatility](/guides/slot-volatility-explained): longer dry spells, harsher sessions and a tiny chance of a huge hit.

Studios do it because big numbers sell. A 100,000x figure stands out in a casino lobby and makes for dramatic stream clips.
