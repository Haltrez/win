Jacks or Better is the plainest video poker game and, on the right paytable, one of the best bets in a casino: a 9/6 "full pay" machine returns 99.54% with optimal play. That return exists only on paper unless you make the right hold on every deal, and the right hold is often not the obvious one. This guide gives you the ranked strategy we use for the hint mode on our [free Jacks or Better game](/table-games/video-poker) and explains the decisions that trip people up.

## Check the paytable first

The game is named after its two variable payouts: 9 for a full house and 6 for a flush, per coin. Casinos lower those two rows to raise their edge, and nothing else on the screen changes.

| Paytable | Full house / flush | Return with optimal play |
|---|---|---|
| 9/6 | 9 / 6 | 99.54% |
| 9/5 | 9 / 5 | 98.45% |
| 8/6 | 8 / 6 | 98.39% |
| 8/5 | 8 / 5 | 97.30% |
| 7/5 | 7 / 5 | 96.15% |
| 6/5 | 6 / 5 | 95.00% |

Also bet five coins. The royal flush pays 800 for 1 at five coins but only 250 for 1 at one to four coins, and that difference is worth about 1.5% of return. If five coins at your denomination is too much, drop to a lower coin value rather than fewer coins.

## The ranked hold list

Find the first line that describes your hand and hold exactly those cards. "High card" means J, Q, K or A.

1. Royal flush, straight flush, four of a kind
2. Four to a royal flush
3. Full house, flush, straight, three of a kind
4. Four to a straight flush
5. Two pair
6. High pair (Jacks or better)
7. Three to a royal flush
8. Four to a flush
9. Low pair (2s to 10s)
10. Four to an outside straight
11. Three to a straight flush with at least as many high cards as gaps
12. Four to an inside straight with three high cards
13. Two suited high cards
14. Any other three to a straight flush
15. Unsuited J-Q-K
16. Two unsuited high cards (with three, keep the lowest two)
17. Suited 10 with a J, Q or K
18. One high card
19. Nothing – draw five

This list gives up about 0.08% against a computer-generated strategy that splits a few of these lines into special cases. For practical purposes it is the strategy.

## The holds that feel wrong

**Break a flush or straight for four to a royal.** A made flush pays 6 and a straight 4. Four to a royal (say J-Q-K-A of spades) is worth about 18.4 coins per coin bet on average, because one card in 47 completes an 800-coin royal and many others still make a flush, straight or high pair. Only a made straight flush or quads outranks it.

**Low pair over four to an outside straight.** The pair (expected value 0.82) beats the open-ended straight draw (0.68) because pairs improve in many ways – two pair pays 2, trips 3, full house 9, quads 25 – while the straight draw has eight outs to a single 4-coin payout. Four to a *flush* (1.22) does beat a low pair, though.

**Three to a royal over four to a flush.** Three suited high cards from 10-J-Q-K-A are worth about 1.41 coins per coin; a four-card flush draw about 1.22. The royal possibility is remote but huge, and the three cards also make high pairs and flushes.

**High pair over three to a royal.** A paying pair of Jacks or better is worth 1.54, more than the three-card royal draw. Take the sure payout.

**Don't hold a kicker.** With a pair, hold the pair and nothing else. An extra ace next to a pair of 8s removes two of the cards that could make you trips or quads and adds very little.

**Keep the lowest two of three unsuited high cards.** With Q-K-A unsuited, hold Q-K and throw the ace. The pair chances are identical, but Q-K leaves more straights open (10-J-Q-K-A and 9-10-J-Q-K) than K-A does.

## Two pair, trips and quads

With two pair, hold both pairs and draw one; the full house (9 for 1) comes one time in 12. With three of a kind, hold the three and draw two – never keep a kicker. With four of a kind you have your payout; the fifth card cannot change it, so it does not matter what you do with it.

## Variance and bankroll

Even with perfect play Jacks or Better swings. Roughly 55% of hands lose, and most of the long-run return sits in rare hands: the royal flush alone contributes about 2% of the 99.54%, and it arrives about once every 40,000 hands. Expect long stretches below your starting balance between the big hits. On a short session the 0.46% house edge is invisible; the variance is not.
