Because many slots come in several RTP versions, the figure in a review isn't always what you'll get. Here's how to check.

1. **Open the game** (real-money or demo mode at the casino you're using – demo mode at a casino usually runs the same version).
2. **Open the menu** – usually a ☰, "i" or "?" icon.
3. **Find the rules / help page**, then scroll to the bottom. Look for "theoretical RTP", "return to player" or "expected payback".
4. **Compare** it with the maximum on our slot page. If our page lists other versions (e.g. 96.5% / 95.5% / 94.5%), you'll see which one you have.

## If it's lower

You don't have to play it. Another licensed casino may run the higher version. Over time the difference adds up – see [RTP explained](/guides/rtp-explained).

## Bonus buy and ante bet RTP

Some games show separate RTP values for the bonus buy or ante bet modes. These are usually within a fraction of a percent of the base value.

## Where providers publish RTP

Not every studio publishes the same information outside the game:

- **Pragmatic Play, BGaming and Nolimit City** publish RTP on their official game pages, and we copy those figures to our slot pages.
- **Thunderkick** shows RTP in the "Game details" box on each game page. Several recent Thunderkick releases list about 94%, which is below the market average – worth knowing before you play.
- **Hacksaw Gaming** publishes RTP for only some games. Where it doesn't, our pages show "Not published" rather than guessing. For a small number of games, two independent databases agree on a figure; we show it with a clear "third-party" label.

## Spotting a lower RTP version

Many games exist in several versions, for example 96.5%, 95.5% and 94.5%. The gameplay is identical; only the long-term cost changes. Signs a casino may run a lower version:

- The info screen shows a figure below the one on the provider's site.
- The paytable shows lower bonus-buy RTPs or a lower "feature" RTP.
- The casino doesn't display RTPs at all – licensed games must show it inside the game, so open the info screen to check.

## What a good RTP looks like

Most modern slots sit between 96% and 96.7% in their top configuration. Anything above 97% is rare – see our [97% RTP slots](/best/97-rtp-slots), led by games like Thunderkick's [1429 Uncharted Seas](/slots/thunderkick/1429-uncharted-seas) at 98.6%. Anything below 95% means you're paying noticeably more per spin over time.

## Quick checklist

1. Find the provider's top RTP on our slot page.
2. Open the in-game info screen at your casino and compare.
3. If it's lower, consider another casino or another game.
4. Remember that RTP is a long-run figure – [volatility](/guides/slot-volatility-explained) decides what happens in one session.
