Every bonus buy slot gives you the same choice: pay a fixed price now, or keep spinning until the bonus turns up by itself. Most advice stops at "buying is risky". This guide puts numbers on both routes using the **bonus frequencies BGaming publishes for 170 of its games**, so you can see what "waiting for it" actually means.

## How often bonuses land on their own

BGaming is one of the few studios that publishes how often the bonus round triggers. Across the 170 slots on our site with that figure:

- The **median is about 1 in 184 spins**.
- Half of them fall between **1 in 150 and 1 in 273**.
- The fastest is [Arrow](/slots/bgaming/arrow) at about 1 in 20; the slowest, [Wild Moon Thieves](/slots/bgaming/wild-moon-thieves), averages 1 in 600.
- 33 of the 170 trigger less often than once every 300 spins.

These are averages over millions of spins. In a real session you can see two bonuses in 50 spins, or none in 800.

## What the natural route costs

At 1 in 184 spins, you'll wager about **184x your stake** on average between bonuses. That sounds worse than a 100x buy, but it isn't the same kind of cost: most of that money comes back as base-game wins along the way. What you actually lose is the house edge on those spins, plus whatever the bonus pays relative to its share of the RTP.

The point is that the natural route spreads the risk over time. Your balance drifts down slowly, with base-game wins topping it up, while you wait.

## What the buy costs

A bonus buy collapses all of that into one click. You pay the price (often 80x to 200x your stake) and get the round straight away. Studios set that price so the **average bonus is worth roughly the price minus the house edge**, which is why buying doesn't change your long-term return by much.

What it does change is the range of outcomes. Many bought bonuses return less than they cost, a few return much more, and there's no base game in between to soften it. Ten 100x buys is 1,000x your stake in a few minutes.

## Volatility: what the data shows

In our database, games with a bonus buy lean heavily towards high risk. Of the 278 slots we've tagged with a bonus buy, **153 are rated very high volatility**. Their median max win is about **10,200x**, against about **5,600x** for games without one. Bonus buys are mostly a feature of games built for big swings.

Among the BGaming games that publish a frequency, those with a buy trigger slightly less often (median 1 in 206 spins) than those without (1 in 157). Rarer bonuses and a buy option tend to come together.

## When each route makes sense

**Waiting for the natural bonus** suits you if you want longer sessions, smaller swings and the base game's own features. It's also the only option in Great Britain and other markets where bonus buys are banned.

**Buying** only makes sense if you've budgeted for it as a set number of buys, not spins, and you're fine with most buys returning less than their price. It doesn't improve your odds; it just speeds everything up.

**Ante bets** sit in between: you pay a bit more per spin to make the bonus more frequent. See [ante bet explained](/guides/ante-bet-explained).
