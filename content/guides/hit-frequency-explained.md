**Hit frequency** (or *hit rate*) tells you how often a slot produces a winning spin on average. It is usually written as "1 in X spins" or as a percentage: a hit frequency of 25% is the same as one win every 4 spins.

It is one of the three numbers that describe how a slot *feels*, alongside [RTP](/guides/rtp-explained) and [volatility](/guides/slot-volatility-explained):

| Number | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| RTP | How much of all money wagered is paid back over millions of spins |
| Volatility | How unevenly that money is paid out – few big wins or many small ones |
| Hit frequency | How often *any* win lands, however small |

## Real hit rates from our database

We store the hit rate whenever a provider publishes it. Across the slots that have one:

- **Nolimit City** games average a win about once every **4.2 spins**, ranging from roughly 1 in 2.5 to 1 in 12.
- **BGaming** games average about once every **3.8 spins**, from 1 in 1.7 to around 1 in 25.

The extremes are revealing. [Wild Clusters](/slots/bgaming/wild-clusters) pays on average every 1.7 spins yet is rated *very high* volatility – most of those hits are tiny cluster wins, while the real value sits in rare big features. At the other end, [Grand Patron](/slots/bgaming/grand-patron) only pays about once every 25 spins.

Nolimit City's [Tombstone R.I.P](/slots/nolimit-city/tombstone-rip) and [Deadwood R.I.P](/slots/nolimit-city/deadwood-rip) pay roughly once every 11–12 spins – a sign of games built almost entirely around rare bonus outcomes. Compare that with [Oktoberfest](/slots/nolimit-city/oktoberfest), an early medium-volatility title that hits about once every 3 spins.

## Hit frequency is not the same as winning

A "win" in hit-frequency terms is any payout, including one that returns *less* than you staked. If you bet 1.00 and win 0.40, that spin counts as a hit even though your balance went down. This is why a slot with a high hit rate can still drain a balance steadily.

What matters for your experience is the combination of numbers:

- **High hit rate + low volatility** – frequent small wins; your balance moves slowly. Good for long sessions.
- **High hit rate + high volatility** – lots of small "consolation" wins, with the real value locked in rare features.
- **Low hit rate + high volatility** – long dry spells punctuated by occasional large wins. Budget accordingly.

## Bonus frequency

Some providers also publish how often the bonus round triggers. Across the BGaming games that list it, the median is around **1 in 180 spins**. That means that in a 100-spin session it is quite normal not to see the bonus at all. Bonus frequency is often more useful than hit frequency when you are deciding how much to budget for a session – see our [bankroll management guide](/guides/bankroll-management-for-slots).
