Progressive jackpot slots are the games with the big, ever-rising numbers at the top of the screen. A small share of every bet feeds a prize pool that grows until someone wins it. They're among the most searched slot types, and among the most misunderstood. This guide explains how progressive pools work, why the base game usually pays back less, and how they compare with the fixed jackpots used in most modern slots, including every jackpot game on Scatter Drop.

## TL;DR

- A progressive jackpot grows with every bet placed on the game, sometimes across many casinos, until one player wins it.
- The funding comes out of every spin, so the base game usually returns less than a comparable non-jackpot slot.
- Fixed jackpots are a set multiple of your bet, such as 25x, 100x or 5,000x, and are far more common in modern slots.
- Scatter Drop focuses on games with published RTP and max win data, so all 120+ jackpot slots we list use fixed jackpots.
- You can try fixed-jackpot games free in demo mode to see how often the tiers appear.

## How progressive jackpots work

1. **Every bet contributes.** A small percentage of each stake goes into the jackpot pool.
2. **The pool grows** with every spin, by any player on the game or the linked network.
3. **Someone wins it.** Usually through a special bonus, a random trigger or a rare symbol combination.
4. **It resets** to a starting amount (the "seed") and starts growing again.

There are three common setups:

- **Standalone:** one machine or one game at one casino feeds its own pool.
- **Local or in-house:** several games at the same casino share a pool.
- **Network or wide-area:** the same game across many casinos feeds one pool. These produce the headline-grabbing amounts.

The catch is simple: a pool can only grow huge because almost nobody wins it. For most players, the jackpot is something they pay towards, not something they collect.

## Progressive vs fixed jackpots

| | Progressive jackpot | Fixed jackpot |
|---|---|---|
| Prize | A cash amount that grows | A set multiple of your bet |
| Changes with your stake? | Often needs max bet or a side bet to qualify | Yes, scales with your bet (1,000x at 1.00 = 1,000.00) |
| Base-game RTP | Usually lower, because it funds the pool | Part of the normal published RTP |
| How often the top prize hits | Extremely rarely | Rarely, but the odds are fixed |
| Typical trigger | Random, bonus wheel or rare combo | Filling a respin grid, rows or a scatter count |

## How to read the numbers

- **RTP** on a fixed-jackpot game already includes the jackpots. On a progressive game, check whether the published figure includes the pool contribution.
- **Volatility** is usually higher on jackpot games, because the top tiers are rare by design and carry part of the return.
- **The jackpot amount** on a progressive game can be tempting, but its size tells you how rarely it's won, not how likely you are to win it.
