Cascading reels are one of the most common mechanics in modern slots. Instead of the reels simply stopping, winning symbols vanish and new ones fall into their place, so a single paid spin can produce a chain of wins. Different studios call it different things (Tumble, Avalanche, Cascades, Super Cascades), but the idea is the same. This guide explains how cascades work, what changes from game to game, and which free demos show the mechanic off best.

## TL;DR

- A cascade removes winning symbols and drops new ones in, so one spin can pay several times.
- Cascades usually pair with multipliers that grow during the chain. That's where the big results come from.
- They don't change the RTP. The chains are already part of the game's published return.

## How cascading reels work

1. **You spin and win.** The game pays the win as usual.
2. **The winning symbols disappear.** Only those positions empty out.
3. **New symbols fall in.** The remaining symbols drop down and new ones fill the gaps from above.
4. **Repeat.** If the new symbols form another win, it pays and the process repeats. The chain ends when a drop produces no new win.

All of this counts as one spin at one stake. That's why cascading games can feel lively even when the stake is low: you often see two or three small wins in a row from one press of the button.

Studios add their own twists:

- **Pragmatic Play's Tumble** in games like [Sweet Bonanza](/slots/pragmatic-play/sweet-bonanza) and [Gates of Olympus](/slots/pragmatic-play/gates-of-olympus) works with pay-anywhere grids, where 8 or more matching symbols anywhere pay.
- **Hacksaw's Super Cascades** in [Ze Zeus](/slots/hacksaw-gaming/ze-zeus) remove every symbol of the winning type, not just the winning group.
- **Nolimit City's collapsing mines** in [Fire in the Hole xBomb](/slots/nolimit-city/fire-in-the-hole-xbomb) open new rows as the chain continues, so the grid itself grows.
- **Thunderkick's Avalanche** in [Esqueleto Explosivo 2](/slots/thunderkick/esqueleto-explosivo-2-reborn) ties each drop to a rising Mucho Multiplier.

## Cascades with multipliers

On its own, a cascade only adds small extra wins. The real value comes when it's combined with a multiplier that grows or sticks during the chain:

- **Running multipliers.** In Gates of Olympus free spins, every multiplier that lands is added to a total that applies for the rest of the round.
- **Spot multipliers.** In [Sugar Rush](/slots/pragmatic-play/sugar-rush), a position that explodes twice starts a multiplier that doubles each time, up to 128x, and in free spins those spots stay for the whole round.
- **Cell multipliers.** [Aztec Clusters](/slots/bgaming/aztec-clusters) activates cell multipliers up to 10x where clusters win.

This is why cascading slots tend to be high or very high volatility: the long chain with a big multiplier is rare, and it carries much of the game's value.

## How to read the numbers

- **RTP** is the long-run share of stakes a game pays back. At 96.5%, the game is designed to return about 96.50 for every 100 wagered over millions of spins. It tells you nothing about one session.
- **Volatility** describes how those returns arrive. Cascading games with growing multipliers are usually high or very high: lots of small chains, and rare long ones.
- **Max win** is the cap on a single round. On cascading games it's usually reached by a long chain with a big multiplier, which is the least likely outcome in the game.

Also note Carnival Queen 2's published RTP of 94.18%, noticeably lower than most games here. Over time, a lower RTP costs more for the same play.

## Playing responsibly

Chains of small wins can make a session feel better than it is: often a "win" in a cascade pays back less than the stake. Set a budget before you start, keep an eye on your balance rather than the win animations, and stop when the budget is used.
